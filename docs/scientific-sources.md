# Scientific Sources

## Primary source

**Practical Manual — Food Chemistry (AS6201)**, Department of Applied Sciences,
Mbeya University of Science and Technology. Supplied with this project at
`FOOD_CHEMISTRY_PRACTICAL_MANUAL_GUIDEBOOK/Practical Manual-Food Chemistry-AS6201.docx`.

This manual is the Level-1 source of truth for every practical, procedure,
formula, hazard note, acceptance criterion and reference reproduced on this
site. Where the website's content differs in wording from the manual, it has
been paraphrased for on-screen presentation but not changed in scientific
substance.

## References cited by the manual itself

These are the literature references the manual cites for its practicals, carried
through to the corresponding practical's Overview tab and reproduced here in full:

- Horwitz W (2000) (editor). *Official Method of Analysis of AOAC International*.
  17th Edition. AOAC International, Maryland, USA.
- WHO (1973). *Report of a Joint FAO/WHO Ad Hoc Expert Committee on Energy and
  Protein Requirements*, WHO Technical Report Series No. 522, WHO, Geneva.
- Greenfield H and Southgate DAT (1992). *Food Composition Data: Production,
  Management and Use*. Elsevier Applied Science, UK.
- Kirk RS and Sawyer R (1991). *Pearson's Composition and Chemical Analysis of
  Foods*, 9th Edition. Longman Scientific & Technical, Essex, England.
- AOAC International (1993). *Methods of Analysis for Nutrition Labelling*,
  Chapter 33 (Sugars — Liquid Chromatographic Methods, 982.14 / 977.20).
- Will RBH and Greenfield H (1984). *Laboratory instruction manual for food
  composition studies*. Department of Food Science and Technology, University of
  New South Wales, p 59.
- Waters (1987). *Choosing the Right Column Chemistry for Carbohydrate Analysis*.
  Notes Food & Beverage, Waters Chromatography Division, Millipore Corporation,
  2:4-6.
- Rashida Rajuva TA & Joy PP (2014). *A Food Technology Lab Manual*. Pineapple
  Research Station (Kerala Agricultural University), Vazhakulam, Muvattupuzha,
  Ernakulam, Kerala.

## Additional source consulted only to clarify a manual-defined concept

- Nielsen SS (editor). *Food Analysis* — consulted only for the general form of
  the titratable-acidity percentage equation used in Practical 3.1, which the
  manual applies directly with its own worked example (7 mL NaOH, 0.1019 N,
  citric acid Eq. Wt. 64.04, 20 mL sample → 0.276% citric acid). This source did
  not add, replace or reinterpret any manual-defined procedure, hazard or
  acceptance criterion — it is cited transparently on the practical's Overview
  tab rather than folded silently into the manual's own content.

No other external sources were used to define scientific content. All hazard
statements, apparatus lists, sample contexts, procedures, formulas and
acceptance criteria elsewhere on the site come directly from the supplied
manual.
